DMCA Notice Procedure

Nekomo respects intellectual property rights and responds to valid notices relating to materials that Nekomo actually hosts. That is limited to this website, its images, and files in the Nekomo-App GitHub repositories.

Content on third-party sites cannot be removed by us. If your work is hosted somewhere else, please send your notice to the host or provider of that site, or to the search engine or platform that links to it. We cannot take down what we do not host, own, or control.

What to include in a notice

To be valid under 17 U.S.C. § 512(c)(3), a notice must be written and include:

  1. a physical or electronic signature of the copyright owner or a person authorized to act on their behalf;
  2. identification of the copyrighted work claimed to have been infringed (or a representative list if multiple works);
  3. identification of the material claimed to be infringing and information reasonably sufficient for us to locate it — for example, the exact URL on this website or the exact file/path in our repository;
  4. your name, mailing address, telephone number, and email address;
  5. a statement that you have a good-faith belief that the use is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law;
  6. a statement, made under penalty of perjury, that the information in the notice is accurate and that you are authorized to act on behalf of the owner of the exclusive right allegedly infringed.

Where to send it

Contact a maintainer through one of these channels. To protect your privacy, please do not post personal details publicly; ask for a private channel first and send the full notice there.

  • Discord — message a server admin or open a ticket, and mention “DMCA notice”.
  • Telegram — message a group admin and mention “DMCA notice”.
  • GitHub issue — open an issue titled “DMCA notice” with the URLs concerned only, and ask for a private channel to send the remainder. You can also use GitHub’s own DMCA process for repository content.

What happens next

We review notices in good faith. If a valid notice relates to material we host, we will promptly remove or disable access to it and may forward the notice (with your details) to the person who posted it. Incomplete notices or notices about content we do not host may receive no action beyond a reply explaining why.

Counter-Notice

If material you posted was removed and you believe that was a mistake or misidentification, you may send a counter-notice through the same channels containing:

  1. your physical or electronic signature;
  2. identification of the material removed and where it appeared before removal;
  3. a statement under penalty of perjury that you have a good-faith belief the material was removed by mistake or misidentification;
  4. your name, address, and phone number, and a statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the federal court for your district (or, if outside the United States, any judicial district in which we may be found) and will accept service of process from the person who submitted the original notice.

We may forward the counter-notice to the original complainant. If they do not notify us within 10–14 business days that they have filed a court action, we may restore the material.

Repeat Infringers

In appropriate circumstances, we will restrict or ban users of our community spaces and contributors who are repeat infringers.

Misrepresentations

Under 17 U.S.C. § 512(f), anyone who knowingly materially misrepresents that material is infringing, or that it was removed by mistake, may be liable for damages. Please make sure your notice is accurate before sending it.
